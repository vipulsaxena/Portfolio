Overview

GoPlay is Gojek's OTT platform — publicly available across Indonesia with exclusive movies and series, including the country's 2019 Academy Awards entry Memories of My Body and originals co-produced with Vice. I joined at the MVP stage as design lead, owning design across every GoPlay surface.

Problem

The challenge

The MVP was built to business requirements without end-user insight, making it hard to use — tasks were slow and unnecessarily prolonged. It also lacked documentation, IA maps, flows and wireframes, and wasn't compliant with Gojek's Asphalt (now Aloha) design system.

The goal

Apply an evidence-based method that involves end-users throughout — designing content discovery that delights, and lifts MAU and minutes watched by at least 25% per quarter.

The search for relevant content is often a failure — an unfortunate consequence of bad content-discovery experiences.

Process

From MVP chaos to evidence-led iteration

Gojek runs a flexible, product-dependent process. With engineering in 3-week sprints, I kept validated designs at least a sprint ahead — then tracked them continuously.

Getting the basics right first

Maintaining design across three platforms was never a solo job, so we made our first hire and scaled with the product. I embedded task management (Asana), documented decisions on Confluence, and turned the engineering flow diagrams into user-flows, IA and wireframes. Then the big one — theming the light Asphalt DLS into a dark mode with GoPlay's hot-pink scheme, and converting the essential components for mobile and web.

Listening to users

To inform product, content and market strategy post-launch, we ran competitive analysis, user interviews and surveys, and mined feedback from social media and the app stores — collecting both quantitative and qualitative data.

Turning insight into bets

With the research in hand, I applied design-thinking to identify multiple “How might we…” statements and ran them through a prioritisation framework — desirability, feasibility and profitability — to decide where to invest.

Iterating discovery through experimentation

I approach design problems by forming a hypothesis and running it through experimentation to conclude with evidence. Three experiments did the heavy lifting.

01

Usability test — Continue Watching

Do users need content details from the CW list? Do they delete finished items? 12 consumers, 45-minute moderated sessions, six alternatives.

Result: Alt #1 — 40% more effective, 23% more efficient. Alt #6 — 66% higher satisfaction.

02

Card sort — Details page & trailers

How should the page be structured, and where do trailers belong? Design reviews + usability picked the layout; an online hybrid card sort set the hierarchy.

Result: Variant #4 won; a similarity matrix produced the final IA.

03

A/B test — Personalised home

Can a personalised home retain users? Presentation layers — feed order, copy, theme-card art — A/B tested by behavioural segment and read via analytics.

Principle: observe what people DO, not only what they SAY.

Continue Watching

Play and details, finally separate

The winning alternative separated the play and details actions and ordered the rail by minutes-left, so returning viewers resume in a single tap.

“The play and details button is separated, nice — I'll watch the content with the fewest minutes left first.”

Details page & trailers

An information hierarchy users voted for

A card-sort–driven IA put the right details first and gave trailers a proper home — so viewers get the “why” before they commit, and shorter trailers keep them curious.

Personalised home

Setting context and tone

Feed order, copy and theme-card artwork are continuously A/B tested by behavioural segment — helping viewers relate to a story's expectation and decide faster.
