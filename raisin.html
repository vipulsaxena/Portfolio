Raisin — Brand Evolution

Brand evolution

Rebrand, rebuild, and a design practice that could keep up.

Two tracks run in parallel — Practice on the product, Enablement for the team. Measured honestly, not just shipped.

The why

Raisin was evolving its brand across product, mobile, and email — without an operating model to make that evolution stick.

The identity had moved on; the experience hadn't. Customers still met Weltsparen in the dashboard, a disconnected app, and emails that felt like another brand. Koto handed over guidelines — not a product system. And design was working without shared research, critique, or conventions. Each gap was survivable alone. Together, they capped how far the rebrand could go.

01

A fragmented product experience

Customers experienced Raisin differently depending on where they landed: a legacy web dashboard, a mobile app that didn't mirror it, and emails that looked like a different brand entirely.

02

A brand handed over, not built out

Brand agency Koto delivered a static identity. After handoff, Raisin's design team had to apply the new visual identity and evolve the entire product experience into a new cohesive brand.

03

A design practice without shared conventions

User studies were rare, static designs were default to showcase and design critiques weren't happening. Every handoff paid a tax for it.

Wealth Hub

Customer problem

Savers landed on a dashboard that still felt like a product catalogue — too much Raisin, too little of their money. They couldn’t orient to what they held, or manage it as their wealth, across savings, investments, and retirement.

The bet

I pushed an asset-class hierarchy as the Wealth Hub spine — not a flat product list. We took it live with the brand evolution across 12 markets.

Savings, Investments, Retirement — one structured hierarchy

Rejected: a flat, single-list dashboard — faster to ship, and favoured internally early on.

Better product, better process

The dashboard needed the controls customers were asking for, and the team needed to stop relying on intuition. I owned the post-MVP backlog — filters, zero-state A/B, Wrapped — ran Raisin's first controlled design experiment, and wrote the ways of working and research playbooks the org still runs on.

I led post-MVP + measurement. We shipped with eng.

Web dashboard · post-MVP

MVP shipped the hierarchy. Customers still had different questions.

Customer problem

The Wealth Hub spine oriented people — but they still couldn’t answer everyday questions: what’s at each bank, what’s by country, what matures next, and what happened this year.

The strategy

I owned the post-MVP sequence — lenses first, then zero-state, then Wrapped — and measured each release against a shared UX framework. We shipped each with engineering.

Impact

3 measured releases · filtering led adoption · zero-state: no conversion lift · Wrapped: engagement + soft AuM

Research · operating model

Wealth Hub proved the value. The team still needed a way to run the next study.

User studies had been rare and German-only. Wealth Hub changed that — multi-market interviews, synthesis, stakeholder alignment. The MVP launch survey showed structured feedback at scale. I authored the research operating model: when to use interviews vs surveys, how participants are recruited through Bloomreach, hypothesis-first briefs, synthesis in Miro, and the compliance layer so legal and markets could sign off once, not per study.

6-step workflow · Bloomreach recruitment · Confluence playbook

Enablement

Made research commissionable outside design.

Before this, running a study meant asking a designer who'd already done one. I documented sourcing, incentives, consent templates, and roles — research lead, stakeholders, decider — so product and growth could initiate validation without reinventing process. Maze was piloted next and became the default for unmoderated testing; the mobile dashboard study ran on that stack.

Maze piloted → default · templates + registry · GDPR-compliant workflow

Mobile and the horizon

The tribe split in two, the mobile redesign had stalled for months, and nobody had written down where the product was going. I took mobile end-to-end — desk research, Maze validation, Cura vision — and raised the team's prototyping bar.

I led mobile IA + vision. I initiated earnings with Deposits. Maze = pre-launch.

Desk research

Read the stall before redesigning.

Interaction data challenged the default “copy web” plan: 42% portfolio-card, 25% donut, 33% tabs — enough to stop blind replication before ideation started.

Web ↔ mobile parity

Re-ground, don't restart.

Took over a stalled programme and pushed mobile-first ideation on what already existed — re-ground the Wealth Hub spine for mobile, don’t clone the layout or throw the system away.

IA + concept

Four tabs, one visual idea.

Designed Overview, Portfolio, Wallet, and Needs Attention as a scannable mobile home — allocation rings, light and dark on the Wealth Hub colour map — ready to validate, not defend.

Enablement

One Data — consistent numbers for stakeholder demos.

Started for mobile stakeholder demos — shared portfolio simulation with Figma variable modes so prototype figures held across tabs, personas, and themes. Later published org-wide as the standard prototyping dataset.

4 persona modes · Figma variable JSON

Maze validation

Pressure-test, don't defend.

Pressure-tested two IAs in a three-week Maze study. Portfolio-card hit 92.9% task success vs 71.4% on the tabbed alternative — enough to ship. Mobile is launching now; this is pre-launch validation, not live impact. Open risk: locating pending products still split 42.9% / 57.1%.
