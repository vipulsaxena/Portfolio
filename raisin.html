Raisin — Brand Evolution

Brand evolution

Raisin is a European savings and investment marketplace — with ~€80bn in assets under management.

I lead B2C design in Growth & Engagement — evolving Raisin’s brand across web and mobile, and the practice that makes multi-market change stick.

The starting point

Raisin was evolving its brand across product, mobile, and email — without an operating model to make that evolution stick.

Identified Gap 01

A fragmented product experience

Diagnosis: On web, customers met a catalogue — not their holdings across categories. On mobile, the same job met a different product and the emails didn't match visual language. One customer, no single home for their wealth.

Identified Gap 02

A brand handed over, not built out

Diagnosis: Brand agency Koto delivered a static identity. After handoff, Raisin design had to scale it across product experience — guidelines existed; a live, multi-market product brand did not.

Identified Gap 03

A design practice without shared conventions

Diagnosis: The rebuild needed a team that could move as one system. What existed was ad hoc — loosely shared craft conventions, a weak research rhythm, and siloed workflows — so every initiative paid a tax, including later work on AI.

· · · · ·

Closing the gaps

Journey

Two programmes, run in parallel — not one tidy sequence.

My remit: close the three gaps — brand evolution into live product (absorb the agency handoff and carry it into the surfaces customers use; redesign web, mobile, and communication into one home for their wealth), and enablement in parallel (ways of working, research and critique rituals, craft standards, and the AI programme) so the team could sustain it. Practice = customer outcomes. Enablement = team capability. I where I owned it; we at tribe scale.

Practice

Research-led decisions, shipped screens, measured outcomes.

Enablement

Rituals, research OS, docs, tooling, coaching — I built; the org adopted.

Impact: NPS, task success, adoption, AuM, and retention all moved on the customer side — alongside research rituals, playbooks, AI workflows, and coaching the org adopted. Measured honestly in Proof; not everything lifted.

01
B2C Growth tribe

A home for their wealth

The web dashboard still behaved like a product catalogue — deposits first, not the customer’s holdings. This chapter turns that surface into a single home for wealth across savings, investments, and retirement, and carries the new brand into a live, multi-market product.

I led Wealth Hub research, IA, and the design bets. We shipped MVP across 12 markets.

Baseline research · 🇩🇪 · One week · 4× designers

Qualitative baseline: Weltsparen through customers’ eyes.

We sat with B2C customers and asked what Weltsparen actually felt like after login — what they needed, what was missing. Not a polish brief; a test of whether a redesign was justified.

Sample set · 8 interviews (±2) · Raisin’s existing B2C archetypes

Customer problem

A catalogue landing, not a home for their money. Savers couldn’t orient to what they held across savings, investments, and retirement.

Orient — consolidated view across asset classes · Monitor — total wealth at a glance · Act — maturities and pending tasks surfaced

Co-creation · two-week sprint · design + product + cross-tribe stakeholders

From scattered insights to one Wealth Hub concept.

Synthesise → HMW → Stakeholder workshops → Converge → One concept

We spent two weeks turning the research into How-Might-We statements. I then ran stakeholder workshops on shared boards — Head of Design, PM, Director, and designers from other tribes — everyone ideating against the same insights and data points, not opinion.

“How might we create a personalized view that adapts to individual user needs?”

“How might we make what’s held at each bank — and how much — visible at a glance?”

“How might we surface maturities and due dates before customers go looking?”

The sessions produced far more ideas than one release could carry. I facilitated the convergence — compiling them into a single coherent concept: one Wealth Hub, organised around what customers hold.

Product design strategy · trade-off 02 · settled in design critique

Catalogue list vs asset-class hierarchy

Organisation was the call critique couldn’t settle. Engineering favoured a flat list to ship faster; I argued for hierarchy by what customers hold.

Key trade-off

A · Flat list

Faster to ship · no asset-class grouping · detail upfront

B · Hierarchy Chosen

Savings · Investments · Retirement — by what customers hold

Position: A flat list reads as a catalogue — the problem customers named. Hierarchy orients by what they hold.

Settled: Design critique — I presented the research case for hierarchy and carried product and engineering with it.

Enablement

I established · design critique culture

Showed designers how to present a clear problem statement and collect feedback productively — not ad-hoc taste. Now a recurring ritual.

Research · operating model

Wealth Hub proved the value. The team still needed a way to run the next study.

User studies had been rare and German-only. Wealth Hub changed that — multi-market interviews, synthesis, stakeholder alignment. The MVP launch survey showed structured feedback at scale. I authored the research operating model: when to use interviews vs surveys, how participants are recruited through Bloomreach, hypothesis-first briefs, synthesis in Miro, and the compliance layer so legal and markets could sign off once, not per study.

6-step workflow · Bloomreach recruitment · Confluence playbook

Enablement

Made research commissionable outside design.

Before this, running a study meant asking a designer who'd already done one. I documented sourcing, incentives, consent templates, and roles — research lead, stakeholders, decider — so product and growth could initiate validation without reinventing process. Maze was piloted next and became the default for unmoderated testing; the mobile dashboard study ran on that stack.

Maze piloted → default · templates + registry · GDPR-compliant workflow

B2C Growth tribe Customer Engagement tribe Tribe split

Mobile and the horizon

The tribe split in two, the mobile redesign had stalled for months, and nobody had written down where the product was going. I took mobile end-to-end — desk research, in-flow prototyping, Maze validation, Cura vision — and raised the team's prototyping bar.

I led mobile IA + Maze validation. I built the in-flow prototype with One Data. Shipping now.

Desk research

Read the stall before redesigning.

Web Wealth Hub earned its structure through customer research. Mobile didn’t have that yet — only a stalled redesign and pressure to copy across. Desk research on legacy interaction patterns (42% / 25% / 33%) showed why that shortcut failed: real usage didn’t match web’s model, and no single mobile pattern had won.

Legacy mobile · interaction data · pre-ideation baseline

Web ↔ mobile parity

Re-ground, don't restart.

Web had earned its structure through research and shipped across 12 markets. Mobile’s job wasn’t to repeat that work — it was to translate it. After desk research ruled out replication, I aligned the tribe on a simple rule: inherit the Wealth Hub concept and system, redesign the mobile surface. That unblocked ideation without discarding what was already validated.

Inherited: hierarchy + colour system · Redesigned: tabs + Home

New mobile IA · asset on platform · insight on Home

Four tabs, one visual idea

Overview, Portfolio, Products, and Wallet — parity with the web Wealth Hub. Home surfaces total assets and performance; Portfolio holds active products. I wired One Data personas into a live prototype so reviews ran on realistic mobile, not flat mocks.

Key trade-off · insight

A · Standalone analytics tab

Dedicated performance surface, separate from holdings

B · Embedded insight

Total assets + performance on Home; owned products on Portfolio

Position: Thin analytics as a primary tab erodes trust on mobile — show wealth and performance where people land; put active holdings on Portfolio.

Enablement

One Data — from mobile prototype to org data standard

What started as persona-matched dummy data for mobile demos became the shared simulation layer — consistent figures across tabs, themes, and markets. Published to Confluence; adopted beyond mobile as Raisin’s user-centric data standard.

4 persona modes · Figma variables · Confluence · org-wide

Maze validation · Unmoderated study · Two-week

Pressure-test the IA

Two-week unmoderated study on the live prototype — portfolio-card vs tabbed IA. Research goal: confirm asset-on-platform Home improves wealth clarity, the Portfolio tab still works for owned products, and primary sections stay legible. Portfolio-card won — 92.9% task success vs 71.4%. Open risk: locating pending products 42.9%.

Enablement

Brought Maze into Raisin — validation loops matter

IPP tribe ran research with Maze after I introduced it — proof that unmoderated validation scales. This study ran on the same prototype stack we used in ideation.

Maze · IPP tribe · research playbook in use

Early signal is strong — app-store reviews are already positive. Design NPS and structured task metrics are still being recorded; this is fresh post-launch, not a closed impact story.
