Case study · OLX Group

OLX Ad Package

16 weeks of design timeline · In development

OLX is monetised through Ad Limits, and one of the primary ways Sellers keep posting more ads is by buying Ad packages.

The updated Ad-package purchasing UX streamlines category selection and provides clearer guidance when buying Ad packages.

Shoutouts

Lorena (UX Writer), Małgorzata (Researcher), Claude (PM), Oana (CPO), Kuba (EM), Monica & Arsen (Jr. PD) — along with the Engineering & BI teams.

Business

Business Objective

Grow the share of monthly paying Sellers through improvements to existing monetisation flows — while contributing to a CSAT goal of +8.0.

Revenue projection

Even a 1% increase in paying Sellers can amount to an additional $110k–$160k in monthly revenue.

Customer Satisfaction

Baseline CSAT the redesign aims to lift toward +8.0.

Build a profitable transactional business ensuring high retention and improving CSAT

Background

Background

As a Seller on OLX you get a few free ads until you're required to pay — with the option to promote Ads to potential Buyers on a 30-day Ad life-cycle.

Research

Initiate research — Jobs to be Done

To begin, I ran 12 B2C & C2C Seller interviews to understand their Desires, Limitations and Catalysts when selling on OLX — with emphasis on the monetisation flows.

When

Sellers (B2C/C2C) choose OLX to sell one or multiple goods and list their Ads.

User

They would like to

Determine & act on how to attract Buyers to their goods.
Optimise the pricing of under-performing goods against the money they invest.

Job step

So that

B2C/C2C — they reach their desired ROI per item.
C2C — they feel they're contributing to sustainability and getting rid of unwanted goods.

Synthesis

We analysed the 24 identified limitations

Post qualitative research, findings were clustered into 24 limitations. A sample of the sharpest:

With: Research Lead, Lead PM, HOD, myself & 2 Jr. Designers.

Sellers can't relate to what personalisation does to their AdsThe personalised-page option wasn't transparent or attractive enough to users.
Statistics feel like a free feature — why pay for it?Sellers assume Ad statistics are always free, so the benefit of paying is unclear.
No freedom to decide when to use Pay-if-you-sellYou first have to use up the pool of free ads to use STR — and Sellers often don't know this.
Hard to find the exact category when buying Ad packagesSellers don't find the category-selection process user-friendly. OpScore 13.12 — the bet.
Sellers don't see the direct results of paying for promotionsPromotion lifts views, but that didn't clearly translate into sales versus re-posting.
Package price is often inadequate to item priceFor cheap items the package price is close to the item's value — the sale is no longer profitable.
STR is only available for OLX Delivery listingsUsers may not know STR exists, especially if their inventory doesn't match OLX Delivery.
…and 17 moreAll 24 limitations were mapped, then quantified and scored next.

Prioritisation

Quantification to prioritisation

I surveyed Sellers on the importance & satisfaction of each problem area, then used Opportunity Scoring to generate an OpScore.

OpScore = Importance + max(Importance − Satisfaction, 0)

Problem

User Problem

Almost 80% of B2C and 90% of C2C Sellers drop at the category-selection page of the buy-package flow.

”
“I like the option to buy an Ad package in advance, but it gets tricky to find the same category in which I post multiple ads.” — Seller, research interview

Audit

Ran a UX audit on the buy-package flow

Explore

Designed 3 possible directions for desktop

Why desktop first? More than 60% of Ad packages are purchased via the OLX website.

#1 Hypothesis

Real-time offer view

Users would appreciate being able to simultaneously browse Ad packages while choosing the category they'd like to post in — making the overall process dynamic.

Offers are shown based on availability. Categories are seen upfront on the offer view.

Two concepts

Two directions, head to head

Design critique to narrow it down to 2 ideas.

9 P&S designers, 4 PMs across pillars, the Engineering team, the P&S HOD and the Data team.

Test

We tested 2 concepts for usability with 10 users

Insights

  • A4/8 struggled to associate dropdown categories with the dynamic offer. Task time was 15% lower than Production.
  • B👑 7/8 were satisfied with the multi-level menu and appreciated the summary view. Task time was 28% higher than Production.
  • AOnly 2/8 found the filter view easier to compare — and they ignored the 'see-more' CTA while comparing packages.
  • B👑 With upfront category tags, 5/8 intuitively named categories as a variable for why price differs per package.

Takeaway

  • →Primary/secondary/tertiary categories got associated with filters — adding cognitive load and failing the hypothesis.
  • →Users found categories with ease; one suggested a shortcut to repeat the selection.
  • →Most users expect offers to carry as much upfront information as possible.
  • →Users don't always tap see-more — a long list of category tags can also add cognitive load.

Outcome

Work in progress

Variant B is in development for an A (Production) / B (Multi-level menu) test on production — Poland first.

During testing, a user suggested a shortcut to repeat the selection. Data showed ~31% of B2C users make a repeat purchase — so we added a shortcut banner surfacing the last purchased Ad package.

A package-recommendation feature is under moderation and rolling out across the Polish market. The same category-selection logic was adopted for mobile.
